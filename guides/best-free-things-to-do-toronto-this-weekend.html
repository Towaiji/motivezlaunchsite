Best Free Things to Do in Toronto This Weekend

The best free things to do in Toronto this weekend include High Park, the waterfront trails, Kensington Market, and free museum nights at the ROM or AGO.

By Ali Towaiji · Published June 10, 2026 · Updated June 12, 2026

No plans, no budget? Toronto has more free stuff to do than most people realize: parks, markets, free museum nights, and waterfront trails. Here's where to go.

Parks and outdoor spaces

High Park is Toronto's biggest green space, and the High Park Zoo inside it is free, yes, including the llamas and capybaras. It's an easy half-day hangout with friends, and there's enough space that even a big group can spread out on a nice day.

Trinity Bellwoods Park in West Queen West is the classic spot for a picnic, frisbee, or just people-watching on a sunny afternoon. It's a short walk from Dundas West and Ossington, both well served by streetcars, so it's an easy meet-in-the-middle for people coming from different parts of the city.

Along the waterfront, Sugar Beach, Cherry Beach, and Woodbine Beach are all free, and the Martin Goodman Trail runs about 22 km along Lake Ontario if you want to walk, bike, or rollerblade. The Toronto Islands themselves are worth a trip too, just budget a few dollars for the ferry, which runs from the foot of Bay Street.

Tommy Thompson Park on the Leslie Street Spit is a quieter option if your group wants nature without the crowds. It's a long, flat trail that's great for biking, and it's mostly free of the weekend rush you'll find at the beaches further west.

Markets and neighborhoods

Kensington Market and the Distillery District cost nothing to wander: vintage shops, street art, and people-watching included. St. Lawrence Market has been around for two centuries and is free to browse (just bring cash if you want to actually eat, a meal from the food stalls typically runs somewhere in the $8–15 CAD range).

Evergreen Brick Works is a former industrial site turned community hub with trails, wetlands, and a farmers' market, a good change of pace from downtown, and it's accessible by TTC bus from Broadview station.

If you're downtown and just want a quick free stop, Nathan Phillips Square in front of City Hall often has something going on: public art installations, skating in winter, or just people-watching with a good view of the Toronto sign.

Free museum nights

The ROM is free on the third Tuesday evening of every month from 4:00–8:30 p.m. through its Third Tuesday Nights Free program, and the AGO is free on the first Wednesday evening of the month from 6:00–9:00 p.m. via First Wednesday Nights, and both require advance tickets booked online. Visitors under 25 can also register for a free AGO Annual Pass for year-round free admission. See our full free museum nights schedule for exact details and booking tips.

Annual free events

Doors Open Toronto, usually held in late May, gives free access to buildings across the city that are normally closed to the public, check toronto.ca each spring for the exact weekend. Afrofest takes over Woodbine Park each summer with free African and diaspora music, food, and culture, and various neighborhood festivals (Taste of the Danforth, Pride, and others) pop up free to attend throughout the warmer months. You typically only pay for food and drinks once you're there.

Free indoor options

If the weather isn't cooperating, the city's library system is an underrated free hangout: the Toronto Reference Library downtown has a large, comfortable atrium and quiet study spaces that work for groups, not just solo studying. Many neighborhood branch libraries also host free events, talks, and exhibits worth checking on their event calendars. The Eaton Centre and similar malls are a fallback if you just want to be indoors and around people without spending anything beyond transit.

What to bring and when to go

For park and waterfront spots, bring water, sunscreen if it's sunny, and a light layer for once the sun starts going down. Toronto evenings cool off faster than you'd expect even in summer. Weekday afternoons tend to be quieter than weekends at the popular parks and markets, so if your schedule allows it, going on a weekday avoids a lot of the crowds. For free museum nights specifically, arriving close to opening time gives you the best shot at getting in and finding parking for bikes or just breathing room inside.

Logistics: getting around without spending much

A single TTC fare covers a one-way trip on subway, streetcar, and bus with transfers, which makes hopping between a park and a market in the same afternoon pretty cheap. If your group is doing a few stops in one day, check whether a day pass makes sense versus paying per ride, it depends on how many trips you're taking. Most of the spots above are within a 10–20 minute walk of a subway station or major streetcar line, so even a "no real plan" weekend rarely means more than one or two transfers.

FAQ

What are the best free things to do in Toronto this weekend?

Free options include High Park and Trinity Bellwoods Park, the waterfront trails and beaches along Lake Ontario, Kensington Market and the Distillery District, St. Lawrence Market, Evergreen Brick Works, and free evenings at the ROM and AGO.

When is the ROM free in Toronto?

The Royal Ontario Museum (ROM) offers free admission on the third Tuesday evening of every month, from 4:00 to 8:30 p.m., through its Third Tuesday Nights Free program. Free timed-entry tickets are released online about two weeks in advance.

When is the AGO free in Toronto?

The Art Gallery of Ontario (AGO) offers free admission on First Wednesday Nights, the first Wednesday evening of each month, from 6:00 to 9:00 p.m. Visitors under 25 can also sign up for a free AGO Annual Pass for year-round free admission.
