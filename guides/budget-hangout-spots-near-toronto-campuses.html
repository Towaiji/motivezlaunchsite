Budget-Friendly Hangout Spots Near Toronto Campuses

By Abdullatif Akhras · Published June 10, 2026 · Updated June 12, 2026

Tight budget, still want to go out? Here are go-to spots near downtown Toronto campuses that won't wreck your week.

Near U of T (St. George campus)

Kensington Market is the obvious pick, a short walk from campus, full of cheap eats (tacos, dumplings, bubble tea), vintage shops, and benches to just sit and hang out. Most snacks and meals in the market land somewhere in the $5–12 CAD range, so a few people can eat for under $20 total. Sneaky Dee's on College St. is a longtime student favorite for cheap nachos and a casual bar atmosphere. For something quieter, Robarts Library's common areas and the green space around Queen's Park are free, central, and easy to meet up at between classes.

Getting there: the St. George campus is served directly by St. George and Spadina stations on Line 1, and Kensington Market is a 10–15 minute walk from either, or a short ride on the College streetcar (Line 506).

Near TMU (Toronto Metropolitan University)

TMU sits right in the middle of downtown, so Yonge-Dundas Square and the surrounding food court options are the default cheap meetup. St. Lawrence Market is a short walk south if you want a more "let's actually walk somewhere" hangout with food stalls to browse. The Toronto Reference Library nearby is a free, comfortable spot with a quiet courtyard for studying or low-key hangouts.

Getting there: Dundas station on Line 1 is right at the edge of campus, and most of these spots are within a 15-minute walk, no transit needed once you're there.

Near York University

York's Keele campus is further from downtown, so on-campus and Vaughan-area spots matter more. Vaughan Mills and nearby plazas have budget food courts, and the campus's own Student Centre common areas are a free fallback between classes. For a downtown trip, the subway connection makes Kensington Market and the waterfront trails (see our free things to do guide) reachable in under an hour.

Getting there: the Keele campus has its own subway stop, York University station, on the Line 1 extension, useful for a quick downtown trip without needing a car or rideshare.

Near Centennial College and Scarborough campuses

If you're further east, Scarborough Town Centre has a large food court with budget options and is connected by bus and the Line 2 extension toward Scarborough Centre station. For something outdoors and free, the Scarborough Bluffs parks (Bluffer's Park and Cathedral Bluffs) are a short bus or rideshare away and make for one of the best low-cost views in the city, bring your own snacks since there's not much for sale on-site.

Classic cheap student bars

Beyond Sneaky Dee's, spots like Duke of Gloucester and The Fox and Fiddle locations near campus areas are known for student-friendly pricing on wings and pints, especially during weekday happy hours, when deals can bring a plate of wings or a pint down to roughly $8–12 CAD. Always double-check current hours, happy hour windows, and any cover charges directly with the venue before heading out, these change often and vary by location.

Free fallback options

When the budget is at zero, lean on free spots: Trinity Bellwoods Park, High Park, and free museum evenings at the ROM (third Tuesday, 4–8:30 p.m.) and AGO (first Wednesday, 6–9 p.m.), see our museum night schedule, are all solid no-cost options that still feel like "going somewhere." If you're under 25, the AGO's free Annual Pass (sign up on ago.ca) means you can drop in any time, not just on free nights.

What to bring (and what to skip)

For most of these spots, a student ID, a transit card or phone with TTC fare loaded, and cash or a card for small purchases is all you need. For parks and beaches, bring water and a layer for when the sun goes down, Toronto evenings cool off faster than the daytime weather suggests, even in summer. For bars, double-check ID requirements (19+ in Ontario) before the group heads out, since getting turned away at the door is a fast way to derail a cheap night.

Splitting costs without the awkward math

One thing that quietly kills budget hangouts is the bill math, figuring out who owes what when everyone ordered something slightly different. If your group goes to the same few spots regularly, it's worth agreeing on a default (split evenly, or everyone pays their own) before you sit down, rather than working it out at the table. It sounds minor, but it's one of those small frictions that makes people hesitate before suggesting a hangout in the first place, especially when money's tight.

When the budget changes week to week

Budgets fluctuate a lot during the semester, fine after a paycheck or OSAP disbursement, tight the week before. Having both a "treat" option (a sit-down meal, a bar) and a free fallback (a park, a free museum night) in your group's regular rotation means you don't have to have the same "where should we go" conversation from scratch depending on who's flush and who isn't that week. The free fallback isn't a downgrade, it's just a different kind of plan that still counts as hanging out.
