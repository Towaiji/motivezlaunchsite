What to Do in Toronto When You're Bored

By Abdullatif Akhras · Published June 10, 2026 · Updated June 12, 2026

It's a random afternoon, you're bored, and nothing's planned. Here's a quick list of things you can actually do in Toronto right now, most of them free or close to it.

Just go for a walk somewhere new

If you've never wandered through Kensington Market or the Distillery District without a plan, that's the move, both are full of small shops, street art, and places to grab a snack. The Martin Goodman Trail along the waterfront is another easy "just walk and see what happens" option, and it stretches far enough that you can pick any direction and find something new.

If you want a change of scenery without going far, the Toronto Islands are a quick ferry ride from downtown, even just riding over and back with no real plan once you're there is a decent way to kill an afternoon. The ferry leaves from the foot of Bay Street, so it's an easy add-on if you're already downtown and not sure what's next.

Another option that's easy to forget: just pick a neighborhood you've never really explored, Leslieville, Junction Triangle, The Beaches, and walk it end to end. You don't need a destination; cafes, small shops, and street art tend to show up on their own, and it's a good way to find a new go-to spot for next time.

Rainy day or too cold to be outside?

Not every "I'm bored" moment lines up with good weather. On a rainy or freezing day, the Toronto Reference Library and most branch libraries are free, warm, and have comfortable seating areas that work for hanging out, not just studying. The Eaton Centre and other downtown malls are an easy indoor walk if you just want to be around people without spending money. And if it's a free museum night (see below), that's an especially good plan for bad weather, you're indoors either way.

Check if it's a free museum night

The ROM is free on the third Tuesday evening of the month (4:00–8:30 p.m.) through its Third Tuesday Nights Free program, and the AGO is free on the first Wednesday evening (6:00–9:00 p.m.) via First Wednesday Nights. If today happens to line up, that's an instant plan, see our full museum night schedule to check. Worth noting: if you're under 25, you can register for a free AGO Annual Pass and skip the free-night scheduling entirely.

Head to a park

Trinity Bellwoods Park and High Park are both reliable for last-minute hangouts, bring a snack, find a bench, and you've got a plan. High Park's free zoo is also a fun curveball if you've got an hour to kill. If you're closer to the lake, Sugar Beach or Cherry Beach work the same way, no entry fee, no booking, just show up.

Browse a market

St. Lawrence Market is open most days and is worth a wander even if you're not buying anything, the food stalls alone are an experience, and a snack there usually runs somewhere around $5–12 CAD. Evergreen Brick Works is a bit further out but worth it if you want trails plus a market vibe, especially on weekends when the farmers' market is running. Both are easy to reach without much planning, St. Lawrence Market is a short walk from Union Station, and Evergreen Brick Works is reachable by TTC bus from Broadview station.

Catch something free that's already happening

Toronto has a steady stream of free public events, outdoor concerts, art installations, neighborhood festivals, especially from late spring through fall. There's no single master list, but a quick search for "free events Toronto this week" or checking toronto.ca's events listings can turn up something happening within walking distance of wherever you already are. Worst case, you've spent five minutes looking and still have all the other options on this list.

Grab something cheap near campus

If you're a student and just want food plus people, check our budget hangout spots guide for reliable cheap eats near U of T, TMU, and York, most of these are a short walk from a subway station, so they work even if you're starting from a random spot downtown.

Make it a "let's just go" plan with friends

Boredom plans tend to work best when they're low-commitment, nobody wants to plan a whole evening when they're just killing time. Texting "I'm bored, anyone want to do X" often gets a better response than "does anyone want to hang out" with no suggestion attached, because it removes the decision-making step for everyone else. Pick one of the options above, name a time that's roughly an hour out, and send it, that's usually enough to turn a boring afternoon into an actual hangout.

FAQ

What can I do in Toronto when I'm bored?

Take a walk through Kensington Market, the Distillery District, or a neighborhood you haven't explored, head to a park like Trinity Bellwoods or High Park, browse St. Lawrence Market, or check if it's a free ROM or AGO night.

Are there free things to do in Toronto right now?

Yes. Parks, the waterfront trails, Kensington Market, the Distillery District, and the Toronto Reference Library are all free any time, and the ROM and AGO each have a free evening once a month.
