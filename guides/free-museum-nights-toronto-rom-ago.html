Free Museum Nights in Toronto: ROM & AGO Schedule

By Ali Towaiji · Published June 10, 2026 · Updated June 12, 2026

Two of Toronto's biggest museums open their doors for free every month, you just need to know when and book ahead. Here's the breakdown.

Royal Ontario Museum (ROM)

The ROM offers free general admission on the third Tuesday evening of every month through its Third Tuesday Nights Free program, from 4:00 p.m. to 8:30 p.m. Free timed-entry tickets are released about two weeks in advance on the ROM's website and tend to go fast, book as soon as they're live, especially if you're coming with a group.

Good to know: special ticketed exhibitions usually aren't included in the free admission, they're open for a small surcharge, so check the current exhibit list on rom.on.ca before you go.

Regular general admission (when it's not a free night) runs in the range of roughly $20–28 CAD depending on age and exhibits, so the free Tuesday window is a real saving if you're working with a student budget.

Getting there: the ROM sits right on Bloor Street, a couple of minutes from Museum station and a short walk from St. George station, both on Line 1. If you're coming from the east or west side of the city, hopping off at either station drops you basically at the door.

Art Gallery of Ontario (AGO)

The AGO offers free general admission on First Wednesday Nights, the first Wednesday evening of each month, from 6:00 p.m. to 9:00 p.m. Free tickets are usually released online at 10 a.m. the Monday before, so set a reminder if you want a guaranteed spot. Details and exact release times are posted on ago.ca.

Outside of that monthly evening, anyone in Ontario under 25 can get free, unlimited general admission to the AGO year-round, but you need to sign up for a free AGO Annual Pass first (it's not automatic at the door). See ago.ca/visitor-information/visit-free for how to register. If you're a student under 25, this is genuinely one of the best "always free" cultural spots in the city.

Getting there: the AGO is a short walk from St. Patrick station on Line 1, and it's close enough to Kensington Market and Chinatown that it pairs naturally with a longer hangout.

Tips for visiting on free nights

  • Book tickets online the moment they're released, both museums cap free entry numbers, and slots can fill up within a day or two.
  • Go early in the free window (right at opening) to avoid the post-work rush, especially for the AGO's 6 p.m. start.
  • Pair it with a nearby free spot, the ROM is a short walk from Yorkville, and the AGO is a few minutes from Kensington Market (see our free things to do in Toronto guide for more ideas in both areas).
  • Free nights are popular for first dates and group hangouts, a good "low commitment, high culture" plan that doesn't require everyone to agree on a budget first.
  • Bring a student ID or transit card if you're coming straight from campus, some discounts and the AGO's under-25 pass require ID on file or at entry.
  • Dress for the weather and expect a short wait outside even with a ticket, since both museums check tickets at the door before letting groups in.

What to actually do once you're inside

If it's your first time at either museum, don't try to "do" the whole building in one evening, the free window is only a few hours. At the ROM, the dinosaur galleries and the Bat Cave are reliable crowd-pleasers if your group has mixed interests, while the Gallery of Korea and the Egyptian Mummies wing are good slower-paced stops. At the AGO, the Group of Seven and Indigenous & Canadian art galleries are a short, satisfying loop if you only have an hour, and the Henry Moore sculpture centre is worth a detour if you like something different from paintings.

A free night is also a low-stakes way to test out a new friend group or a first hangout with people from class, there's always something to point at and talk about, and nobody has to commit to a sit-down meal if the vibe isn't there.

Other free or discounted museum options

Beyond the ROM and AGO, look out for the Gardiner Museum's pay-what-you-can evenings and rotating free days at smaller institutions like the Bata Shoe Museum and Museum of Toronto. Doors Open Toronto (usually late May) also gives free access to dozens of buildings citywide for one weekend a year, keep an eye on toronto.ca for the exact dates each spring.

Turning a free night into an actual plan

The hardest part of a free museum night usually isn't the museum, it's getting a group to actually agree on a date and show up. Once you know the third Tuesday (ROM) or first Wednesday (AGO) is coming up, save it as a plan in Motivez, add the friends you want there, and let everyone vote on what happens afterward, food in Kensington Market, a walk through Yorkville, or just heading home. Having the date and the "after" plan in one place cuts out a lot of the back-and-forth that normally kills these kinds of last-minute culture nights.

FAQ

When is the ROM free?

The Royal Ontario Museum offers free general admission on the third Tuesday evening of every month, from 4:00 p.m. to 8:30 p.m., through its Third Tuesday Nights Free program. Free timed-entry tickets are released online about two weeks in advance and go fast.

When is the AGO free?

The Art Gallery of Ontario offers free general admission on First Wednesday Nights, the first Wednesday evening of each month, from 6:00 p.m. to 9:00 p.m. Free tickets are usually released online at 10 a.m. the Monday before.

How can I get free museum admission in Toronto if I'm under 25?

Anyone in Ontario under 25 can register for a free AGO Annual Pass for year-round, unlimited general admission. Toronto Public Library cardholders can also book a free Museum + Arts Pass covering several major museums and galleries.
